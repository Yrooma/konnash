Reading in a Second Language: A Decade of Evidence

A. Researcher · B. Scholar — illustrative text

This review surveys a decade of research on how adults read in a second language. It asks which instructional approaches are associated with gains in comprehension, and how consistently those gains are reported across settings.

1 Introduction

Reading in a second language draws on vocabulary, grammar and background knowledge at once, and the balance between them shifts as a reader gains experience. Earlier reviews have tended to examine one approach at a time.

Here we bring the evidence together, so that teachers and researchers can see where the findings agree and where they do not.

2 Method

We searched five databases for studies published between 2012 and 2022. Of the 412 studies screened, 138 met the criteria for a full review. Each study was read and coded independently by two readers, and disagreements were resolved by discussion.

Most of the studies took place in university settings, and few reported follow-up beyond one semester. Sample sizes ranged widely, from small classroom groups to national cohorts.

3 Findings

Across the included studies, approaches that combined extensive reading with explicit vocabulary teaching were most often associated with gains, although the size of the gains varied with the measures used.

Studies that reported delayed tests found that part of the early advantage faded, which suggests that long-term effects deserve more attention than they have received.
